Our view of education is a structured map given to us from childhood. Going to school, getting nice grades, choosing a career, getting a degree, and then landing a job. This map gives us directions, a set path to follow.

But there is a problem with this that I would like to highlight: we often follow this map blindly, without acknowledging our position within it.

We follow rules, memorize equations, pass tests. And yet, fail to question where am I? Why am I here? Do I really want to be here? What is the destination this path is leading to?

For example, many of us choose a particular degree not because we have discovered a passion for it, but because the path is clearly marked as secure, prestigious, or expected by others; we follow directions successfully only to question whether the destination was ever our choice.

Sometimes we become so focused on the endpoint \- an exam, a degree, a job. That we fail to look around and understand the landscape we are traveling through. 

It may be that we are looking at education the wrong way. Perhaps an acute definition of education lies in us acknowledging the freedom to know our position, choose our own path, question its destination, and even draw new ones if existing ones don't make sense.

A map is only useful when you know where you are. If you don't, even the most detailed map can simply become a set of directions you blindly follow.

After all, what good is a map if we don’t know where we stand?
